Antiques Collection in Australia

A story of colonial trade, regional wealth, and generational memory.

Total Historical Object Collections

Proportional symbols show registered collection sizes. Victoria and New South Wales dominate national holdings. They hold the vast majority of artifacts.

Where Australians Visit Museums

Public museum attendance varies sharply by state. The Northern Territory leads nationally. New South Wales and Victoria record lower rates.

Dot Density of Artifact Records

Scattered markers reveal historical clustering. Artifacts concentrate in early coastal settlements. This highlights early colonial expansion.

Composition of Antiques

Ceramics dominate the recorded inventory. Textiles and Metalware form the next largest shares. Furniture, Jewellery, and Clocks make up the remainder.

Artifact Density by Era

The heatmap shows dominant category-era combinations. Victorian and Gold Rush ceramics form the largest concentration. Other eras show significantly lower density.

How Categories Shifted

This slope chart tracks category ranks across eras. Ceramics rise sharply in prominence. Conversely, furniture declines in relative volume.

Volume by Decade

This chart breaks down artifacts by decade. 1850s and 1920s periods show the highest volume. Later decades see fewer registered items.

Volume and Mean Age

Production peaked in the Victorian and Gold Rush eras. Surviving artifact volume drops in later decades. Consequently, their average age naturally decreases.

Comparing Attendance

Connected dots compare visual arts and crafts. Every state shows higher craft participation. The gap is widest in Western Australia.

Engagement by Age

Females aged 35–44 show the highest overall engagement. Males follow a similar trend but consistently record lower numbers. Engagement for both sexes drops sharply after age 75.
